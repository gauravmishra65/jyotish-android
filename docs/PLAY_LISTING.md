Most astrology apps want your birth details on their server. This one keeps them on your phone. Gaurav Jyotish makes your Kundli, checks Kundli Milan, finds a good Muhurat and reads your numerology — in English or Hindi, fully offline.

★ WHAT IT CALCULATES, AND BY WHOSE RULES

• Vedic (Parashari): 16 divisional charts, D1 to D60. Six dasha systems — Vimshottari, Ashtottari, Yogini, Chara, Kalachakra, Narayana. Shadbala strength, yogas with how rare each one is, upagrahas and chara karakas.
• Your choice of ayanamsa: Lahiri, Raman, KP (Krishnamurti), True Chitra, Fagan-Bradley or De Luce. Most apps pick one and never tell you.
• Kundli Milan: Ashtakoota out of 36 gunas, the ten Porutham of South Indian practice, and Mangal Dosha checked from Lagna, Moon and Venus — with cancellations stated, not hidden.
• Lal Kitab: its own chart and remedies, read by Lal Kitab's rules rather than Parashari's.
• Varshaphal (Tajika): the annual chart with the year's own dasha.
• Prashna: the chart of the question, for when the birth time is unknown.
• Western: seven house systems — Placidus, Koch, Whole Sign, Equal, Regiomontanus, Campanus, Porphyry. Transits, midpoints, harmonics, draconic charts, and a Hellenistic layer with sect and Firdaria.
• Chinese: BaZi four pillars with Na Yin, the twelve growth phases, favourable elements and structure. Zi Wei Dou Shu in two schools. Feng Shui flying stars, including replacement charts.
• Numerology: Pythagorean, Chaldean and Kabbalah schools, Mulank and Bhagyank, the Lo Shu grid and your Kua number.
• Muhurat: checked against tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana, weekday, lagna and Rahu Kaal, plus Tarabala and Chandrabala for the people involved.
• Also: birth-time rectification, and Tamil, Kollam, Bengali, Nepali and Thai calendars.

★ WHY THIS APP IS DIFFERENT

• Every reading names its source. Each section says which tradition's rules produced it. When Vedic and Western disagree, you see both and know why, instead of one blended answer from nowhere.
• One engine, many traditions. All of it computed from the Swiss Ephemeris on your phone — not a mix of lookup tables and approximations.
• It won't pretend to know. No predictions of death, illness or divorce. Readings describe tendencies, and say so.
• It says when it isn't sure. If the birth time is approximate or unknown, the reading tells you which parts to trust less.
• Hindi that reads like Hindi, not machine-translated English. Switch language at any time.

## Editions and selling

| Edition | File | How it opens fully |
|---|---|---|
| Demo | `app-demo-release.apk` (shared directly) | Unlock code for the buyer's email (`tools/licence/`) |
| Full | `app-full-release.apk` (sent to buyers directly) | Already open |
| Play | `app-play-release.aab` (upload to Play) | In-app product `full_unlock`, bought through Google Play |

All three share the application ID `com.gauravmishra.jyotish` and the signing key, so one installs over another and
keeps saved Kundlis. In Play Console: create a one-time in-app product with the ID `full_unlock`, set its price,
and add licence testers to try a purchase before release. The Play listing must say "In-app purchases: yes".

## Data safety form

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Does the app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **No** | Birth details, names and places, and the optional registration (name, email, phone), are stored only in the app's private storage on the phone. After registering, the person may choose to open their own email app with their name, email and phone filled in, addressed to the author; they send it themselves, and the app transmits nothing. |
| Is data sent off the device? | No | The app has no INTERNET permission; the engine and the place list are bundled. |
| Is data encrypted in transit? | Not applicable | Nothing is transmitted. |
| Can users request that data be deleted? | Yes, on the device | Deleting a profile removes it and its cached chart; uninstalling removes everything. |
| Backups | Excluded | `allowBackup="false"` and data extraction rules exclude cloud backup and device transfer. From v16 the person can make a password-encrypted backup file (AES-256-GCM) and save it themselves through the system file picker; the app uploads nothing. |

Permissions requested: none in the demo and full editions. The Play edition adds Google Play Billing (the BILLING permission); Play itself handles payment and keeps the purchase history, which the data-safety form should list as "Purchase history, collected, not shared, required for the purchase". Other third-party SDKs: none (no analytics, crash reporting or ads).

## Before uploading

1. The Play edition is signed with the private upload key when the repository secrets
   `JYOTISH_UPLOAD_KEYSTORE_B64`, `JYOTISH_UPLOAD_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD` and `JYOTISH_UPLOAD_KEY_ALIAS` are set (the CI log
   says "signed with the private upload key"). Without them it falls back to the committed test key. The demo and
   full APKs always use the test key so they install over earlier copies. Raise `versionCode` for every upload.

   **As of 1.0.0 those secrets are not set on this repository.** A `workflow_dispatch` run with `release=true`
   now refuses to build rather than hand over a bundle Play will reject, and the release step prints the
   certificate the bundle carries. Two ways out:

   - Set the three secrets once, at Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions. `JYOTISH_UPLOAD_KEYSTORE_B64`
     is the keystore run through `base64 -w0`. After that every release run produces an uploadable bundle.
   - Or sign on the machine that holds the keystore: `tools/release/sign-aab-with-upload-key.ps1` strips the
     test signature from a built bundle and re-signs it, prompting for the password locally. Nothing about the
     keystore leaves that machine.

   Either way, confirm the certificate against **App integrity → App signing → Upload key certificate** in Play
   Console before uploading. A mismatch is refused at upload with no useful explanation.
2. Enrol in Play App Signing.
3. Upload `mapping.txt` from the same build, so crash reports are readable.
4. Screenshots: the CI run's `ci-results` branch has current ones (Home, Kundali, Milan, Muhurta, Numerology in
   English and Hindi).
5. Privacy policy URL: Play requires one even when nothing is collected; the data-safety table above is the
   content it needs.
