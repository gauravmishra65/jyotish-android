What is my Nakshatra?

Your nakshatra is the segment of sky the Moon stood in when you were born — one of twenty-seven, each 13°20′ wide. Enter the moment of your birth and it is worked out below, on your own device.

Only the moment matters here, not the town — the Moon is in the same place in the zodiac for everyone on Earth at a given instant. The time zone is what fixes that instant.

How this was worked out

The Moon's position comes from the ELP-2000/82 lunar theory, and the sidereal zodiac from the Lahiri ayanamsa — the one the Government of India's calendar reform committee settled on, and the default in most Indian practice. The ayanamsa used for your date is shown with your result.

Checked against Swiss Ephemeris over 5,000 random dates between 1900 and 2050: mean difference 11 arcseconds, worst case 28. The comparison runs against the same Swiss Ephemeris data files the Android app ships, so the site and the app are measured against one reference rather than each against its own. A nakshatra is 48,000 arcseconds wide, so that margin only matters within a hair of a boundary — and when your Moon falls that close, this page says so instead of guessing.

Where your birth details go

Nowhere. The calculation runs in this browser tab. No date, time or zone is sent anywhere, stored anywhere, or logged anywhere — there is no server here to send it to.

You do not have to take that on trust. Open your browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab, and press the button. You will see no request leave.
